## Italian
Core ingredients: tomato, basil, garlic, olive oil, parmesan, mozzarella, pasta, risotto rice, oregano, balsamic, mascarpone, prosciutto, pancetta, ricotta, pecorino.
Signals: pasta, pizza, risotto, gnocchi, focaccia, biscotti, tiramisu, calzone, bruschetta, lasagna, ravioli, tortellini, cannoli, panna cotta — tomato-herb sauces, cured meats (prosciutto, pancetta), mascarpone-based desserts.
  Pasta signals: carbonara, bolognese, marinara, pesto, alfredo, cacciatore, piccata, marsala → Italian even with non-Italian protein.
Format rules: biscotti / tiramisu / calzone / lasagna / bruschetta / garlic bread / risotto / pizza dough → Italian REGARDLESS of modifier or protein swap.
  (Gluten-Free Biscotti → Italian; Twinkie Tiramisu → Italian; Buffalo Chicken Calzone → Italian)
  Exception: explicit foreign cuisine prefix overrides the format rule.
  (Mexican Lasagna → Mexican; Mexican Pizza → Mexican; Taco Lasagna → Mexican)
Confused with: American — a hot pasta/ravioli dish is Italian; a COLD mayo pasta salad is American picnic food.
  Cajun/ranch/BBQ/taco pasta → American (pasta is just the vehicle).
Trap: don't call every dish with "pasta" in the name Italian; check if it's a warm Italian preparation vs. an Americanized salad.
  "Naan pizza" → Italian (pizza format wins over naan).
  Mac and cheese, pasta salad, buffalo/ranch/BBQ pasta → American, not Italian.

## Mexican
Core ingredients: corn/flour tortilla, chili, lime, cilantro, cumin, avocado, beans, queso fresco, masa, tomatillo, green chile, huitlacoche, epazote, chipotle.
Signals: tacos, enchiladas, guacamole, mole, tamales, quesadillas, nachos, fajitas, burritos, tostadas, carnitas, salsa, pico de gallo — with genuinely Mexican fillings/spices.
  Key ingredient signals: green chile, tomatillo, huitlacoche, salsa-verde, mole → strong Mexican markers.
Format rules: taco / enchilada / burrito / quesadilla / nacho / tostada / fajita → Mexican REGARDLESS of modifier.
  (Tofu Tacos → Mexican; Impossible Burrito → Mexican; BBQ Pulled Pork Nachos → Mexican)
  Enchilada / burrito / taco casseroles and layered Mexican dips → Mexican even if named "easy casserole."
  Exception: explicit foreign cuisine modifier overrides taco format → Other.
  (Chicken Teriyaki Tacos → Other; Korean BBQ Tacos → Other)
  Exception: fry bread / Navajo taco → American (Native American, not Mexican).
Confused with: American (Tex-Mex overlap), and Other when a taco or burrito shape holds an Asian/European filling.
Trap: a taco/burrito/nacho SHAPE does not automatically make it Mexican — judge by the filling AND the modifier.
  Latin America is NOT Mexican: Cuban, Brazilian, Peruvian, Argentine empanadas, chimichurri → Other.
  Ceviche → Other unless explicitly in a Mexican context.

## Chinese
Core ingredients: soy sauce, ginger, garlic, sesame oil, rice wine, scallion, hoisin, oyster sauce, tofu, bok choy, five-spice, Sichuan peppercorn, black bean sauce, rice vinegar.
Signals: stir-fries, fried rice, lo mein, egg rolls, wontons, dumplings, potstickers, dim sum, General Tso's, orange chicken, kung pao, Mongolian beef, beef with broccoli, chow mein — with soy-ginger-sesame base.
Format rules: egg rolls / wontons / lo mein / dumplings → Chinese REGARDLESS of protein or modifier.
  American-Chinese restaurant food → always Chinese: General Tso's, Orange Chicken, Beef with Broccoli, Bourbon Chicken.
  (Keto Beef Egg Roll Slaw → Chinese; Shrimp Wontons → Chinese)
  Fried rice / stir-fry: use ingredient signals — if protein/spices are distinctly American (turkey, BBQ) or foreign-marked (Thai, Korean, Japanese), that signal wins.
  (Leftover Turkey Fried Rice → American; Kimchi Fried Rice → Other; Thai Fried Rice → Other)
  Exception: Hawaiian/local fusion marker overrides → Other. (Local Kine Wontons → Other)
Confused with: other East-Asian cuisines (Japanese, Korean, Thai, Vietnamese) — they all share soy/ginger base.
Trap: do NOT default every East-Asian or soy-based dish to Chinese.
  Teriyaki / miso / dashi → Japanese → Other.
  Gochujang / kimchi → Korean → Other.
  Fish sauce / lemongrass → Thai/Vietnamese → Other.
  "Dumpling" alone does NOT mean Chinese — American chicken & dumplings (biscuit-dough) → American.
  Leftover / turkey / BBQ fried rice → American, not Chinese — American protein modifier overrides fried rice format.

## Indian
Core ingredients: garam masala, turmeric, cumin, coriander, ghee, lentils (dal), chickpeas, paneer, curry leaf, cardamom, chili, fenugreek (methi), mustard seeds, tamarind, besan (chickpea flour).
Signals: curry, dal, biryani, tikka, masala, samosa, naan, roti, chapati, paneer, tandoori, korma, vindaloo, saag, bhaji, pakora, lassi, kulfi, halwa, chikki — subcontinental masala/ghee/garam-masala base.
  Strong signal: warm-spice + lentil/legume base + ghee → Indian even without "curry" in the name.
Format rules: samosa / biryani / dal / lassi / tikka / naan / chapati / roti → strong Indian defaults.
  Pakistani, Bangladeshi, Sri Lankan dishes fall under Indian for classification purposes.
  (Vegetable Samosas → Indian; Chicken Biryani → Indian; Paneer Tikka → Indian)
  These are strong defaults not hard overrides — check for fusion signals (wonton wrapper samosas may lean Other).
Confused with: Other (Thai/Middle Eastern) when spices overlap — cardamom, cinnamon, honey, nuts appear in both Indian and Middle Eastern dishes.
Trap: "Curry" alone is NOT Indian — Thai/coconut-lemongrass/Japanese/Caribbean curry → Other.
  Only subcontinental masala/ghee/garam-masala curries → Indian.
  Shared spices (cardamom, cinnamon, honey, nuts, rosewater) do NOT determine cuisine alone —
    baklava uses cardamom + honey + nuts but phyllo pastry format is Greek/Middle Eastern → Other, not Indian.
  Lamb patties / kebabs with cumin + coriander + mint + garlic but NO garam masala/ghee/curry base → Other (Middle Eastern), not Indian.

## American
Core ingredients: generic US pantry — ground beef, cheese, potatoes, corn, BBQ sauce, bacon, canned soups (cream of mushroom), mayo, Velveeta, Worcestershire sauce, ranch dressing, hot sauce.
Signals: burgers, BBQ, mac & cheese, casseroles, cold pasta salads, sandwiches, sliders, meatloaf, pot roast, sheet-pan dinners, chili, cornbread, biscuits, fried chicken, cheesecake, brownies, banana bread, muffins, apple pie, coleslaw, granola, cereal, oatmeal.
Format rules: misleading place names → American: French Toast, German Chocolate Cake, Swiss Steak, Italian Wedding Cookies.
  Regional styles → all American: Southern, Cajun/Creole, New England, Tex-Mex, Hawaiian, Midwestern comfort food.
  Default rule: everyday desserts, baked goods, drinks, snacks, sauces with NO foreign signal → American.
  Cajun / Creole → always American.
  Hawaiian → American (Hawaii is a US state): kalua pork, Hawaiian rolls, spam fried rice.
  Stroganoff (Americanized, egg noodles + cream of mushroom) → American unless explicitly "Russian."
  Goulash (macaroni-tomato-beef US style) → American unless explicitly "Hungarian."
  Chicken and Dumplings (biscuit-dough style) → American, not Chinese.
  These are strong defaults — explicit foreign nationality marker in name overrides (Russian Stroganoff → Other).
Confused with: Other — model incorrectly defaults unmarked Western dishes to Other instead of American.
Trap: generic US home cooking with NO foreign-cuisine signal is American, NEVER Other.
  Everyday drinks, snacks, desserts, baked goods with no foreign signal → American, never Other.
  Do NOT use Other because you are unsure — if torn between two of the five, pick the most probable.

## Other
Core ingredients: varies by sub-cuisine — fish sauce/lemongrass (Thai/Vietnamese), teriyaki/miso/dashi (Japanese), gochujang/kimchi (Korean), feta/phyllo/tzatziki (Greek), tahini/za'atar/sumac (Middle Eastern), butter-cream-wine (French), sauerkraut/spaetzle (German/Eastern European), scotch bonnet/allspice/jerk (Caribbean).
Signals: sushi, ramen, pho, pad thai, bibimbap, bulgogi, gyoza, kimchi, miso soup, baklava, quiche, strudel, crepe, pierogi, schnitzel, jerk chicken, empanadas, ceviche, paella, moussaka, tagine, shawarma, falafel — any dish clearly outside the five cuisines.
  Thai/Vietnamese: fish sauce, lemongrass, galangal, coconut milk + lime, Thai basil, rice paper.
  Japanese: teriyaki, miso, dashi, sushi, panko, wasabi, sake, mirin.
  Korean: gochujang, kimchi, doenjang, bulgogi, bibimbap.
  Greek: feta, phyllo, tzatziki, pita, lemon + oregano.
  Middle Eastern: tahini, za'atar, sumac, harissa, rose water, pomegranate molasses.
  French: butter-cream-wine sauces, quiche, crepe, baguette.
  German/Eastern European: sauerkraut, spaetzle, pierogi, schnitzel, paprika-heavy goulash.
  Caribbean: scotch bonnet, allspice, jerk seasoning, plantains.
Format rules: baklava / phyllo pastry / quiche / strudel / crepe / blintz → Other REGARDLESS of filling or spice modifiers.
  (Easy Baklava → Other; Mini Quiche Lorraine → Other; Apple Strudel → Other)
  Explicit nationality marker in dish name → Other: Russian / Swedish / Danish / Polish / Korean / Vietnamese / Japanese / Thai / Filipino / Greek / French / German / Hungarian / Irish / British.
  (Swedish Meatballs → Other; Korean BBQ Meatballs → Other; French Onion Soup → Other; Hungarian Goulash → Other)
  Fusion dishes with no clear dominant cuisine → Other.
Confused with: Chinese (East-Asian overlap), Mexican (Asian-filled taco shapes), American (defaulting unmarked dishes to Other instead of American).
Trap: use Other ONLY when the dish clearly belongs to a cuisine outside the five — NEVER because you're unsure between two of the five.
  If torn between two of the five, pick the most probable of {American, Italian, Indian, Mexican, Chinese}.
  Latin America is NOT Mexican → Other (Cuban, Brazilian, Peruvian, empanadas, chimichurri).
  "Curry" alone is not Indian — Thai/Caribbean/Japanese curry → Other.
  Hawaiian is the exception — goes to American, NOT Other.
  Stroganoff / Goulash → American unless explicitly "Russian" or "Hungarian" in the name — do NOT default these to Other.
